Nice has a way of staying with you long after you leave. The light there is different. It falls soft and golden over the old town, and even a simple walk to get bread feels like a small event. I miss it a lot, especially now that autumn is creeping in and the memories feel even warmer by comparison.

Anik Boutik is the first thing that comes to mind. That little shop had such a specific charm. It was not big or flashy, but it felt personal, like the kind of place where every item was chosen with care. Stepping inside felt like a quiet pause in the middle of a busy day. I still think about it and wish I could just wander back in.

That is really the spirit of Nice though. The small stores everywhere give the city its heartbeat. You turn a corner and find a tiny shop selling soap, or spices, or handmade jewelry, and the owner is usually right there, happy to chat. It never feels corporate. It feels like real life is happening right in front of you, unpolished and warm.

And then there was the bike race. Watching it live was something else entirely. The energy on the streets was contagious. People lined up early, some with flags, some with coffee, everyone just buzzing with excitement. When the riders finally came through, the speed and focus on their faces was incredible. It only lasted a few seconds, but the crowd's roar stayed in the air much longer. Events like that make a city feel alive in a way that photos never quite capture.

The beach deserves its own little mention too. Nice does not have soft sand, it has stones, but somehow that makes it even better. The sound of the waves rolling over the pebbles is calming in a totally different way. Lying there with the Mediterranean stretching out in front of you, the color shifting from turquoise near the shore to deep blue further out, is one of those simple pleasures that never gets old. Even just sitting there with a coffee, watching people swim or paddle board, felt like enough.

What surprised me most, and honestly delighted me, was how kind people were. Locals were patient even when my French was shaky, waiters cracked jokes, shop owners gave small tips about hidden spots, and strangers on the street were happy to point me in the right direction with a smile. That kind of everyday warmth adds up. It makes a trip feel less like tourism and more like a short stay among friends.

Then there is the airport tram, which honestly deserves some appreciation too. Getting back felt so easy and stress free. No dealing with taxis or traffic jams, just a smooth tram ride straight to the terminal. It gave me extra time to look out the window and watch the city slide by one last time, palm trees, old buildings, glimpses of the sea, all fading gently as the airport got closer. It felt like a proper, calm goodbye instead of a rushed scramble.

Thinking back now, it is the little things that stick the most. The bell on the door of Anik Boutik. The smell of fresh pastries drifting out of a bakery. The rhythmic sound of pebbles under my feet at the beach. The cheers during the race. The tram gliding along the coast one final time.

Nice has this rare balance of being lively and relaxed at the same time. It never feels rushed, yet there is always something happening if you look for it. That mix is hard to find, and it is probably why the city stays in my mind so often.

I really hope to go back soon. There is something about that place that makes ordinary days feel special, and I would love to feel that again, even just for a few days. Until then, I will keep replaying those small moments in my head, the shop, the shore, the race, and that easy tram ride home.
