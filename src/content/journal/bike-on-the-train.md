A cycling blog that only talks about the saddle misses half of touring in Europe: **the train**.

You ride the good roads. You skip the industrial park and the six-lane. You put the bike in the luggage car (or hang it, or book the bike ticket), sit down, and start the next stage where the landscape gets interesting.

## Practical, from the German side

- Book a **Fahrradkarte** when the train needs one. Peak ICEs are fussy; regional trains are often easier.
- Arrive early. Doors and bike spaces fill.
- A small soft bag is easier in a vestibule than a touring fortress with four panniers — but panniers work if you are patient.
- Lights and a lock for the station.

Ferries are the same idea: bike in, coffee, then you roll off into another country.

This is still cycling. The kilometres you ride should be the ones worth riding. The train is a tool, like a pump.
